# J08 historical time-of-day and forecast coverage assessment

Research owner: Jubilee Model Research & Calibration. Coordination: existing Jubilee Dot. Date: October 14, 2026, America/Chicago. Research-only assessment; no operational change or predictive-skill claim.

## Finding

Dawn is a defensible operational focus for the documented sample, but it is not a complete description of Jubilee occurrence. Of the 16 canonical confirmed events, 14 have some morning/predawn timing support, one spans late afternoon through evening/night, and one has unknown clock timing. These are evidence-inventory counts, not population frequencies, onset probabilities, forecast accuracy or a recommendation to change schedules. Two of the 14 rely on relayed/summary timing rather than independently reopened original time evidence.

The strongest demonstrated gap is target definition: the current three-day outlook explicitly says its intraday validity window was not specified by the prior heuristic. A dawn publication time is not a forecast validity interval. Therefore neither a dawn-only hit rate nor an all-day missed-event rate can currently be calculated defensibly. The 1950 event establishes that a dawn-only target excludes at least one documented occurrence, not that an actual forecast failed or cameras would have missed it.

## Evidence and classification method

Baseline: main `21964eb13c281398f550c96addf4f5c3d89ad99b`; event_history blob `0c3611e9bd4226983427488b52ff3b702eff937e`. PR #88 independently read as merged. All 16 rows were inspected; no event labels or source records were edited.

The mutually exclusive event-level partition is: morning/predawn-supported; documented nonmorning multi-period; unknown. Period tags preserve crossings: morning includes explicitly reported early morning/predawn; daytime means afternoon when distinct from morning; evening and nighttime follow explicit source language or approximate reported clock spans. These descriptive labels do not establish sunrise/sunset or a historical UTC offset. A predawn morning can also be dark; do not interpret the partition as an illumination classification. No event is counted twice in the 16-row total.

Onset, observed activity, harvest, message receipt, publication and cessation are different timestamps. Only source-supported values are retained. A date inferred from weekday/calendar is not an inferred time of day. An unknown end time does not establish that activity stopped at dawn or noon. A known four-hour duration does not locate an event on the clock.

## Demonstrated gaps versus unproven failures

1. **Occurrence-domain gap:** September 17, 1950 is demonstrably outside a dawn-only target. On an analogous present-day in-season date, the configured canonical slots would include evening opportunities (e.g. 18:06, 19:06, 20:06 and 22:06). That counterfactual does not prove a usable camera view, successful capture, biological recognition or alert.
2. **Forecast evaluation gap:** exact start/end and outcome definition are absent from the current artifact. Specify in a separately reviewed research contract whether the target is new onset, any ongoing Jubilee activity, or remaining harvest opportunity in a defined shoreline cell and interval. Do not silently rewrite production scope or score prior forecasts against a newly chosen interval.
3. **Lead-time gap:** a dawn+25-minute assessment can occur after predawn onset. A correct description of an event already underway is detection/nowcast, not advance warning. Lead time requires archived issue time, event onset bounds, input availability and notification time separately.
4. **Sampling and reporting limitations:** hourly/two-hour canonical spacing can leave brief events between analyzed bursts; poor darkness/geometry can make biology UNKNOWN. The 10–16 cloud-synthesis gap and evening feedback gate limit timely synthesized/user-facing coverage. These are documented design limitations; this sample establishes no actual missed modern event attributable to them.
5. **Validation coverage gap:** the opportunistically assembled, historically heterogeneous 16-event sample and predawn-scoped controls cannot estimate an all-day event frequency, sensitivity, specificity, false-alert rate or calibrated probability. Adjacent 1971 days, shared news lineages and clustered camera views are not fully independent. No-report silence is not a clean negative.

## Further historical work: material value and stopping rule

Yes, targeted timing research would materially improve evaluation eligibility and distinguish advance prediction from recognition of existing events. It would not, by itself, improve demonstrated predictive skill.

- **First: modern exact-window positives.** Recover already-held original source/capture timestamps, onset/last-active/end bounds and location for 2020, 2024, 2025 and August 2026. August 29 pocket timing must remain separate; do not merge August 30 Montrose lights with the Fairhope biological event. These dates offer the best potential overlap with environmental data. Existing missing August camera archive remains missing, not assumed recoverable.
- **Second: scientific and archival ambiguity.** Inspect May's full August 26 measurement narrative/table for clock times, and resolve 1959 July 7/8 chronology from existing source holdings. Recover onset bounds for short 1972 and 2013 cases where possible. Preserve unknowns when source text does not answer the question; do not contact institutions again without specific authorization.
- **Third: out-of-dawn scope.** Extract timing from already recovered Loesch/Mobile Press material, deduplicate against the admitted 1950 case, and seek source-specific nonmorning examples only where they change coverage assessment. Do not reopen the retired UGA deer-log lead or claim to resolve 35/37.
- **Prerequisite for scoring:** Research and Dawn owners propose an explicit target-window specification for review, while leaving production unchanged. Only then match eligible event intervals and independently observed controls; use episode/season holdouts and available-at checks. Interval-uncertain events remain uncertain or receive a separate sensitivity analysis, never an invented exact onset.

Stop broad archival expansion when recovered details do not change window membership, lead-time bounds, environmental matching or observation-effort assessment. Better-targeted timing and usable controls have greater evaluation value than additional date-only positives.
