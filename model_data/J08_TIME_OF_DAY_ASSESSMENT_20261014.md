## Finding

Dawn remains a defensible operational focus, but does not cover the full documented occurrence domain. The 19 canonical events partition into **15 morning/predawn-supported, three nonmorning/multi-period, and one clock-unknown**. The 15 comprise 13 source/report-supported rows (including the brief retrospective June 13, 1947 wording) and two relayed/summary rows. Source/report support is not exact-clock verification or equal evidence quality. These are inventory counts, not population frequencies, onset probabilities or forecast performance.

The additions strengthen a substantive **evening/overnight validation gap**, not evidence of a newly diagnosed operational failure. June 14 adds late-afternoon/about-dark activity; August 5–6 adds an evening-to-next-day episode with a cumulative catch reported by 03:00. A dawn-only assessment cannot establish coverage of the earlier evening onset or overnight harvest, even if activity persists into the next day. Sensing outside dawn already existed in the October 14 inspection. Coverage must distinguish forecast target, actual sampled visibility/biology and notification timing.

The forecast-validity problem remains: the inspected outlook did not specify intraday bounds. Issue time is not validity time. No dawn-only hit rate, whole-day missed-event rate or improvement in predictive skill follows from expanding the denominator. No new production or runtime inspection is claimed in this reconciliation.

The original 16-row analysis remains in PR #91 commit `b7126e81cf03300531a4b8932f0c0b1732e2ecb6`. Canonical event labels and source records are unchanged by this reconciliation.

The mutually exclusive event-level partition is: morning/predawn-supported without an explicit evening-to-next-day episode; documented nonmorning/multi-period; unknown. Explicit evening/cross-midnight episodes take precedence over incidental morning activity, so August 5–6 is not double-counted as a morning row. Period tags preserve crossings: morning includes explicitly reported early morning/predawn; daytime means afternoon when distinct from morning; evening and nighttime follow explicit source language or approximate reported clock spans. These descriptive labels do not establish sunrise/sunset or a historical UTC offset. A predawn morning can also be dark; do not interpret the partition as an illumination classification. No event is counted twice in the 19-row total.

Onset, observed activity, harvest, message receipt, publication and cessation are different timestamps. Only source-supported values are retained. A date inferred from weekday/calendar is not an inferred time of day. An unknown end time does not establish that activity stopped at dawn or noon. A known four-hour duration does not locate an event on the clock.

## Demonstrated gaps versus unproven failures

1. **Occurrence-domain gap:** June 14, 1947 and September 17, 1950 establish non-dawn activity; August 5–6, 1950 adds evening/overnight activity before next-day continuation. On an analogous present-day in-season date, the configured canonical slots would include evening opportunities (e.g. 18:06, 19:06, 20:06 and 22:06). That counterfactual does not prove a usable camera view, successful capture, biological recognition or alert.
2. **Forecast evaluation gap:** exact start/end and outcome definition are absent from the current artifact. Specify in a separately reviewed research contract whether the target is new onset, any ongoing Jubilee activity, or remaining harvest opportunity in a defined shoreline cell and interval. Do not silently rewrite production scope or score prior forecasts against a newly chosen interval.
3. **Lead-time gap:** a dawn+25-minute assessment can occur after predawn onset. A correct description of an event already underway is detection/nowcast, not advance warning. Lead time requires archived issue time, event onset bounds, input availability and notification time separately.
4. **Sampling and reporting limitations:** hourly/two-hour canonical spacing can leave brief events between analyzed bursts; poor darkness/geometry can make biology UNKNOWN. The 10–16 cloud-synthesis gap and evening feedback gate limit timely synthesized/user-facing coverage. These are documented design limitations; this sample establishes no actual missed modern event attributable to them.
5. **Validation coverage gap:** the opportunistically assembled, historically heterogeneous 19-event sample and predawn-scoped controls cannot estimate an all-day event frequency, sensitivity, specificity, false-alert rate or calibrated probability. Adjacent 1971 days, shared news lineages and clustered camera views are not fully independent. No-report silence is not a clean negative.
